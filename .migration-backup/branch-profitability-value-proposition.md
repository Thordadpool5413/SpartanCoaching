# Why Your Census Number Is Not Arbitrary — And Why Nobody Told You

## What Nobody Teaches Hospice Sales Reps About the Business They Sell For

---

### The Number You Were Given Without an Explanation

At some point early in your hospice sales career, someone handed you a goal. Maybe it was eight admissions a month. Maybe it was twelve. Maybe it was phrased as "we need to hit an ADC of 60 by Q3."

You nodded. You went to work. And if you are honest, you never fully understood where that number came from, why it mattered so much, or what happened to the branch financially when you fell short.

That is not your fault. Nobody told you.

Your sales leader was focused on hitting the number, not explaining it. The clinical team was focused on patient care, not branch economics. The executive director knew the pressure but rarely translated it into something a rep in the field could understand and care about.

This document exists to close that gap. Because when you understand why the number matters, the work changes. The urgency becomes real. The mission becomes clearer. And you become a better rep.

## Part One: What a Hospice Branch Actually Costs to Run

Most sales reps think of their branch as a care delivery operation. It is also a business — one with a fixed cost structure that does not pause when census is low.

Before a single patient is admitted, before a single referral is made, the branch is spending money every month. Here is what that looks like in real terms.

---

### Clinical Staff — The Core of the Cost Structure

The clinical team is the heart of hospice care. It is also the largest expense category a branch carries. These positions are not optional. They are required by Medicare Conditions of Participation, and they must be paid whether the branch has 20 patients or 80.

| Role | Annual Salary (Approximate) |
|---|---|
| Executive Director | $140,000 |
| Supervisor RN Case Manager | $110,000 |
| RN Case Manager (x2 minimum) | $100,000 each |
| Hospice Aide (x2 minimum) | $50,000 each |
| Social Worker | $75,000 |
| Chaplain | $70,000 |
| After Hours RN | $95,000 |
| Weekend RN | $95,000 |
| Medical Director (Contract) | $75,000 |

As the census grows, so does the clinical team. RN Case Managers are typically added for every 12 patients. Hospice Aides scale at roughly every 8 patients. The branch does not get to choose — it has to staff to the caseload or it falls out of compliance and quality suffers.

---

### Operational Staff — The Infrastructure Behind the Care

Care coordination requires people who never touch a patient directly but without whom the operation collapses.

| Role | Annual Salary (Approximate) |
|---|---|
| Intake Coordinator | $60,000 |
| Secretary / Office Administrator | $55,000 |

These roles handle referral intake, scheduling, documentation, supply orders, and the administrative machinery of keeping a branch functional. They are fixed costs from day one.

### Sales and Marketing — The Rep Who May Not Know the Number

| Role | Annual Salary + Benefits (Approximate) |
|---|---|
| Sales Rep / Marketer | $115,000 |

This is you. And here is the part that matters: your salary is a fixed cost that the branch pays regardless of your production. Every month you are employed, that cost hits the budget. The branch needs your production to justify that line item — and to cover every other line item on this list.

---

### Per-Patient Daily Costs — What Each Admission Actually Costs to Serve

Beyond salaries, every patient in census generates a daily cost. These are the clinical supplies, medications, and services consumed in the course of providing routine hospice care.

| Cost Category | Daily Cost Per Patient (Approximate) |
|---|---|
| Pharmacy / Medications | $22 – $44 |
| Durable Medical Equipment (DME) | $10 |
| Medical Supplies | $10 – $23 |
| Clinical Travel | $6 |
| Other Patient-Related Costs | $5 |
| **Total Daily Variable Cost** | **$53 – $88 per patient, per day** |

These are conservative estimates. High-acuity patients — those with advanced cardiac disease, ALS, or complex pain management needs — can push daily costs significantly higher.

---

### Monthly Overhead — The Cost of Having a Building and Running a Business

In addition to salaries and patient costs, every branch carries a layer of fixed operational overhead. Rent, utilities, liability insurance, billing software, compliance training, office supplies, and a dozen other items that exist before anyone walks in the door.

Typical monthly overhead for a small to mid-size hospice branch: **$35,000 – $45,000 per month.**

Annualized, that is **$420,000 – $540,000** in overhead — money spent before you count a single salary or serve a single patient.

## Part Two: Where the Revenue Comes From

Medicare pays hospice under a per diem model called Routine Home Care (RHC). For each day a patient is in hospice and receiving care at home, the branch receives a fixed daily rate from Medicare.

As of recent rate schedules:
- **Days 1–60 of a spell:** approximately $208 – $224 per patient per day
- **Days 61 and beyond:** approximately $177 – $209 per patient per day

This rate is what a branch receives for every patient, every day, on census. It does not matter whether it was a low-touch day or a high-acuity day with multiple clinical visits — the payment is the same.

After subtracting the daily patient costs (pharmacy, DME, supplies, travel), the **contribution margin** — the amount each patient day actually contributes toward covering salaries and overhead — is roughly **$120 – $155 per patient per day** depending on acuity and length of stay.

That number is the engine. Every patient on census is generating that much daily toward covering the fixed costs of the branch.

---

## Part Three: The Break-Even Number — What It Is and Why It Is Not Optional

**Break-even ADC** is the minimum Average Daily Census a branch must maintain for its revenue to exactly cover all of its costs — salaries, per-patient costs, and overhead combined.

It is not a growth target. It is the survival line.

Below break-even ADC, the branch loses money every single day. The losses are real, they compound, and at a certain point they threaten the financial viability of the entire operation.

A typical hospice branch requires somewhere between **35 and 55 patients on census at all times** just to break even — before generating a dollar of profit.

Here is what that means in practical terms: if a branch has a break-even ADC of 45 and is currently sitting at 32, it is burning through cash every month. The gap between 32 and 45 is not a gap in numbers. It is a gap in eligible patients who are not getting care — and a gap in financial health that threatens the branch's ability to keep its doors open.

---

## Part Four: The Target Margin ADC — The Real Sales Goal

Break-even is not the mission. Break-even means the branch survived the month. Healthy hospice organizations operate at an **operating margin of 12 – 18 percent**.

**Target Margin ADC** is the census level where the branch achieves its operating margin goal. This is the number a rep should actually be working toward — not just survival, but sustainable, investable performance that allows the organization to hire better, train more, expand to new markets, and serve more patients.

The difference between break-even ADC and target margin ADC is typically **15 to 30 additional patients on census**. Every admission a rep converts and every patient who stays on service longer than expected moves the branch closer to that target.

## Part Five: What This Means for You in the Field

Now that you know the cost structure, read these situations differently.

**When your manager says "we need three more admissions this month,"** they are not talking about a quota. They may be talking about keeping the branch above break-even. Three admissions, assuming an average length of stay of 90 days, adds roughly 7–8 patients to the running ADC over a quarter. That could represent $150,000 or more in annual contribution margin.

**When a referral stalls and you let it go,** the cost is not just a missed commission. It is a patient who did not get care, and a gap in the census that the branch has to absorb financially while still paying every salary on that list above.

**When a patient is admitted with a short prognosis and stays only two weeks,** the revenue generated barely covers the admission cost. The patients who drive branch sustainability are those with longer lengths of stay — patients who were referred early, when hospice could still provide months of meaningful support.

**When you understand that every conversation you have in the field is connected to the financial engine of the branch,** the work becomes less transactional and more purposeful. You are not just moving referrals. You are keeping the branch viable so it can continue to serve the community.

---

## Part Six: The Admissions Math — What You Actually Need to Produce

To maintain a given ADC, a branch must continuously replace patients who have died or discharged. The formula is straightforward:

> **Monthly Admissions Needed = (Target ADC × 365) ÷ Average Length of Stay ÷ 12**

For a branch targeting 60 ADC with a 90-day average length of stay, that is approximately **24 admissions per month** — roughly 6 per week — just to hold census flat.

If a branch has two marketers splitting that production, each rep needs to close approximately **12 admissions per month** to keep the branch at its census target.

That is not a soft goal. That is the math.
