# THE SPARTAN SALES MODEL

## A Complete Operating System for Hospice Sales Performance

**Spartan Coaching | 2026 Edition**

---

## Executive Summary

The Spartan Sales Model is a disciplined, patient-first operating system for hospice growth. It connects strategy, field execution, coaching, and financial accountability in one repeatable model.

The model is built on a simple belief: hospice sales teams do not become consistently productive through personality, charisma, or a higher volume of unplanned visits. They become productive when the organization gives representatives a clear territory strategy, a coachable conversation process, measurable activity standards, an accountable daily workflow, and an honest view of the economics behind every admission.

The Spartan Sales Model answers six management questions:

1. **Where should the representative spend time?**
2. **What should happen during each referral-source conversation?**
3. **What activity level is required to achieve the admission goal?**
4. **How does the representative turn a relationship into a clear referral pathway?**
5. **What does the representative cost, and when does that investment break even?**
6. **How should a manager coach performance without rewarding empty activity?**

The system combines four connected layers:

- **The Spartan Method:** the philosophy and standards that govern the work.
- **Healthcare Sales Mastery:** the four-subject conversation model of Discovery, Connecting, Guiding, and Commitment.
- **The Field Operating Rhythm:** territory planning, account preparation, practice, visits, debriefs, and next steps.
- **The Measurement System:** activity targets, conversion, admissions, rep cost, contribution, and branch economics.

The result is visible work that another person can understand, repeat, measure, and coach.

## 1. Purpose of the Model

Hospice referral growth is often treated as a relationship business with few reliable standards. Representatives are told to “build relationships,” “get in front of more people,” or “stay top of mind.” Those instructions sound reasonable, but they do not define what good work looks like.

The Spartan Sales Model replaces ambiguity with a shared operating language.

It does not reduce hospice care to a transaction. It does the opposite: it gives teams enough structure to protect patient choice, support clinical judgment, reduce referral friction, and help appropriate patients reach care sooner.

The model exists to create:

- Consistent and appropriate referral pathways.
- Better preparation before every account interaction.
- Clear measures of activity and outcomes.
- Faster identification of performance gaps.
- Coaching based on observable behavior rather than opinion.
- Financial visibility into the true cost and value of a sales representative.
- A repeatable system that does not depend on one exceptional personality.

### What “Spartan” Means

Spartan means disciplined commitment to a higher purpose. In practice, that means:

- Prepare with intent.
- Practice under pressure.
- Measure progress in the open.
- Correct quickly.
- Finish strong.
- Honor patient choice.
- Support clinical judgment.
- Protect privacy.
- Prove progress in the customer’s numbers.

## 2. The Three Pillars

Every part of the model rests on three pillars: Discipline, Empathy, and Strategy.

### 2.1 Discipline

Discipline is structure applied consistently. It means the representative knows which accounts matter, why each visit is scheduled, what outcome is expected, and what happens next.

Discipline includes:

- Weekly territory planning with named priority accounts.
- Standard call preparation.
- Deliberate objection practice.
- Consistent visit and follow-up protocols.
- Tracking activity and outcomes instead of vanity metrics.
- Reviewing performance and correcting the next repetition.

Discipline does not mean rigid scripting. It means removing avoidable uncertainty so the representative can be more present in the actual conversation.

### 2.2 Empathy

Empathy is the ability to understand the referral source’s clinical responsibilities, operational pressures, communication preferences, and concerns.

Empathy includes:

- Listening before presenting.
- Understanding the contact’s workflow and constraints.
- Using plain language.
- Recognizing that different roles experience different barriers.
- Building trust over time instead of forcing transactional wins.
- Keeping the patient’s dignity, choice, and needs at the center.

A hospital case manager late on Friday has different needs from a physician early Monday morning. Effective hospice sales recognizes the difference.

### 2.3 Strategy

Strategy is purposeful allocation of time and attention. It prevents activity from becoming random motion.

Strategy includes:

- Territory analysis and segmentation.
- Account prioritization by patient impact and referral potential.
- Competitive and market intelligence.
- Routing that protects selling time.
- Clear weekly win conditions.
- Resource allocation based on evidence.

The strategic question is not “How many places did we visit?” It is “Did we spend enough quality time with the right referral sources to create the required outcomes?”

## 3. Non-Negotiable Ethical Standards

The Spartan Sales Model is governed by ethical boundaries that apply to every subject, account, conversation, note, report, and coaching session.

1. **Patient choice is honored at every step.** The patient and family retain the right to choose their provider.
2. **Clinical judgment is supported, never replaced.** Sales may educate and reduce process friction, but clinical decisions remain with qualified clinicians.
3. **Privacy is protected through behavior.** Representatives use only approved systems and appropriate information.
4. **Only minimum necessary data is used.** The system should not collect patient details merely because it can.
5. **Access belongs to named, accountable users.**
6. **Protected information never leaves approved systems.**
7. **The model supports appropriate access to care.** It is not a mechanism for pressuring inappropriate referrals.

The practical rule is simple: account planning and coaching should focus on referral-source behavior, workflow, education, objections, and next steps—not patient-identifying information.

## 4. Healthcare Sales Mastery: The Four-Subject Model

The conversation model progresses through four subjects:

**Discovery → Connecting → Guiding → Commitment**

These subjects are run in sequence because skipped steps create weak relationships and vague follow-up. Each subject has a purpose, an execution standard, and a measurable output.

### 4.1 Subject One: Discovery

**Purpose:** Understand the contact, the account, the workflow, and the conditions required for referral confidence.

The representative learns:

- The contact’s role in identifying, discussing, or initiating hospice referrals.
- What the contact sees as an appropriate hospice trigger.
- How decisions move through the account.
- Where the current process slows down or fails.
- What concerns make a hospice conversation feel risky.
- What information the contact needs.
- How and when the contact prefers communication.
- What a successful hospice partner would make easier.

**Execution standard:** Ask focused questions, listen for operational and emotional friction, and confirm understanding in plain language.

**Measurable output:** A completed contact profile that identifies needs, workflow, decision role, concerns, preferences, and referral-confidence conditions.

**Failure pattern:** Presenting services before understanding the account.

### 4.2 Subject Two: Connecting

**Purpose:** Establish a useful, credible working relationship based on the contact’s stated needs and preferences.

Connecting is not generic rapport. It is alignment.

The representative:

- Adapts communication to the contact’s role and style.
- Agrees on a useful cadence.
- Delivers information in a form the contact can use.
- Demonstrates reliability through specific follow-through.
- Connects the hospice’s support to the account’s real workflow.

**Execution standard:** Make each interaction relevant, timely, and easy to act on. Do not use the same pitch for every role.

**Measurable output:** A documented working agreement covering communication method, cadence, useful content, and the next meaningful interaction.

**Failure pattern:** Mistaking friendliness or repeated drop-ins for progress.

### 4.3 Subject Three: Guiding

**Purpose:** Help the contact connect hospice capabilities to a specific clinical or operational problem.

The representative translates capabilities into useful outcomes:

- Clarifies how hospice supports the patient, family, and care team.
- Addresses misconceptions and risk concerns.
- Explains what happens after the call.
- Removes process friction.
- Uses relevant examples without exposing protected information.
- Confirms that the contact can explain the value in their own words.

**Execution standard:** Guide rather than pressure. Use the contact’s stated needs to frame the solution.

**Measurable output:** The contact can articulate a concrete use case for the hospice and explain why the pathway is useful.

**Failure pattern:** Delivering education that never becomes operationally relevant.

### 4.4 Subject Four: Commitment

**Purpose:** Turn understanding into a clear referral action.

Commitment defines:

- The clinical or situational trigger.
- Who initiates the call.
- How the referral is made.
- What information is needed.
- What the hospice does next.
- How follow-up will occur.

**Execution standard:** Remove ambiguity. Walk through the pathway until the contact can describe it without needing additional instruction.

**Measurable output:** A verbal or documented referral pathway naming the trigger, caller, method, required information, and follow-up process.

**Failure pattern:** Ending with “I’ll check back” instead of an agreed next action.

## 5. Territory and Account Strategy

The Spartan territory model directs effort toward the accounts where disciplined work can improve patient access.

### 5.1 Segment the Territory

Accounts should be evaluated using practical factors such as:

- Current referral volume.
- Potential appropriate-patient volume.
- Strength of current relationship.
- Access to decision-makers and referral influencers.
- Competitive position.
- Known process friction.
- Strategic fit with the hospice’s capabilities.
- Travel time and route efficiency.

A simple A/B/C structure can be used:

- **A accounts:** high impact or high potential; receive the strongest preparation and most intentional cadence.
- **B accounts:** meaningful opportunity requiring development.
- **C accounts:** lower immediate potential, maintenance, or qualification needed.

The category should influence effort, not become a permanent label. Accounts move when evidence changes.

### 5.2 Name a Weekly Win Condition

Every week should have one clear win condition. Examples:

- Complete Discovery with the primary case-management contact at a target hospital.
- Establish the referral trigger and call process with a skilled nursing team.
- Resolve a preferred-provider objection and secure a next-step meeting.
- Re-engage three dormant high-potential accounts.
- Confirm a working agreement with a new physician practice.

The win condition creates focus. It also gives the manager a clear coaching target.

### 5.3 Build the Route Around Priorities

The route should protect selling time and support the weekly win condition.

For each planned account, identify:

- Why this account matters now.
- Which subject is active.
- Who the representative needs to reach.
- The desired outcome.
- The likely objection or friction.
- The next-best action if the primary contact is unavailable.
- The follow-up commitment to secure.

Unplanned proximity is not a strategy. A nearby low-value stop should not displace a prepared high-value interaction.

## 6. The Daily Field Operating Rhythm

The Sales Command Center organizes work into one continuous account loop:

**Queue → Pre-call → Practice → Visit → Debrief → Next Step**

### 6.1 Queue

Select the next account using territory priority, open commitments, timing, route, and current subject.

The queue should answer:

- What must happen today?
- What is overdue?
- Which account has the highest-value next action?
- Which planned interaction supports the weekly win condition?

### 6.2 Pre-call

Prepare the interaction before entering the account.

The representative reviews:

- Account and contact context.
- Current subject in the four-subject model.
- Desired outcome.
- Questions to ask.
- Relevant value message.
- Likely objections.
- Specific next step to request.

### 6.3 Practice

Rehearse the difficult part before the visit. Practice may focus on:

- A cold opener.
- A clinical-workflow question.
- A preferred-hospice objection.
- “We are not ready” or timing resistance.
- A transition from education to commitment.
- A concise request for the next step.

Practice is specific to the account and outcome. It is not a generic script exercise.

### 6.4 Visit

Execute the planned conversation while remaining responsive to the contact.

The representative should:

- Lead with relevance.
- Listen for new information.
- Stay within the active subject.
- Confirm understanding.
- Ask for an explicit next step.
- Protect privacy and never record patient-identifying information in unapproved tools.

### 6.5 Debrief

Immediately record what happened:

- Was the contact reached?
- What was learned?
- Did the intended subject advance?
- What objection appeared?
- What commitment was made?
- What should be repeated or changed?
- What is the one “tiny edge” for the next attempt?

### 6.6 Next Step

Close the loop before moving on.

Every meaningful interaction should produce:

- A scheduled follow-up.
- A promised resource.
- A practice assignment.
- A new contact to engage.
- A defined referral-pathway action.
- Or a clear decision to deprioritize the account.

The core command is: **Schedule → prepare → close the loop.**

## 7. Activity Model: Turning Admission Goals Into Daily Work

The activity model converts an admission goal into the number of live referral-source conversations required.

### 7.1 Define an Activity

One activity is one live conversation with a contact at a referral source. Emails sent, materials dropped off, miles driven, and attempted visits may support the work, but they are not substitutes for a live conversation.

### 7.2 Establish Conversations per Admission

For a tenured representative:

**Conversations per Admission = Last-Cycle Conversations ÷ Last-Cycle Admissions**

When the representative lacks reliable history, the model uses the team baseline:

**Team Baseline = 15 conversations per admission**

### 7.3 Calculate the Monthly Target

**Base Conversations = Round Up(Monthly Admission Goal × Conversations per Admission)**

The model adds a protection buffer:

**Buffer Conversations = Monthly Admission Goal × 2**

**Monthly Conversation Target = Base Conversations + Buffer Conversations**

The buffer acknowledges cancellations, access problems, timing differences, and normal field variability.

### 7.4 Convert Monthly Activity to Weekly and Daily Targets

Using 20 working days per month and five working days per week:

**Weekly Conversation Target = Round Up(Monthly Target ÷ 4)**

**Daily Conversation Target = Round Up(Monthly Target ÷ 20)**

The representative should know the daily target before the week begins.

### 7.5 New-Hire Ramp

New representatives build toward steady-state activity:

- **Week 1:** 50% of weekly target.
- **Week 2:** 70%.
- **Week 3:** 85%.
- **Week 4:** 100%.

The ramp is not permission to avoid accountability. It is a structured progression that gives managers time to install quality while activity increases.

### 7.6 Day Capacity

The planning model protects time for:

- Live conversations.
- Travel.
- Documentation.
- Lunch.
- Preparation and follow-up.

Travel time is estimated within a practical range based on the day’s target. Conversation, driving, and documentation blocks should be planned before the route begins.

## 8. Conversion and Funnel Mathematics

The rep-cost model links field activity to referrals and admissions.

### 8.1 Calls and Referrals

**Monthly Calls = Calls per Day × Working Days per Month**

**Annual Calls = Monthly Calls × 12**

**Monthly Referrals = Monthly Calls ÷ Calls per Referral**

**Annual Referrals = Monthly Referrals × 12**

### 8.2 Referrals and Admissions

**Monthly Admissions = Monthly Referrals × Referral-to-Admission Conversion Rate**

**Annual Admissions = Monthly Admissions × 12**

**Lost Admissions = Referrals − Admissions**

Lost admissions are not automatically a sales failure. Some referrals will be clinically ineligible, choose another provider, or not convert for legitimate reasons. The measure is a prompt for review: Where is the process losing appropriate patients, and what can the team improve?

### 8.3 Conversion Loss

The model assigns a cost signal to unconverted referrals:

**Cost per Referral = Annual Fixed Rep Cost ÷ Annual Referrals**

**Conversion Loss = Lost Admissions × Cost per Referral**

This is a management indicator of effort invested without an admission outcome. It is not a claim that every non-admission should have converted.

## 9. The Full Cost of a Sales Representative

Base salary is only one component of the investment.

### 9.1 Fixed Rep Cost

The model includes:

- Base salary.
- Benefits load.
- Mileage reimbursement.
- Other fixed costs such as technology, training, materials, management allocation, or approved overhead.

**Benefits Cost = Base Salary × Benefits Load Percentage**

**Mileage Cost = Annual Business Miles × Configured Mileage Rate**

**Benefits and Fixed Additions = Benefits Cost + Mileage Cost + Other Fixed Costs**

**Annual Fixed Rep Cost = Base Salary + Benefits and Fixed Additions**

**Monthly Fixed Rep Cost = Annual Fixed Rep Cost ÷ 12**

The mileage rate is an explicit model assumption and should be updated when the organization changes its reimbursement policy.

### 9.2 Commission Cost

Commission is based on modeled monthly admissions and the applicable tier.

The current standard tier structure is:

- **1–10 monthly admissions:** $100 per admission.
- **11–20 monthly admissions:** $125 per admission.
- **21 or more monthly admissions:** $150 per admission.

Organizations should replace the tiers with their actual plan.

**Monthly Commission = Monthly Admissions × Active Tier Rate**

**Annual Commission = Monthly Commission × 12**

**Total Annual Rep Cost = Annual Fixed Rep Cost + Annual Commission**

### 9.3 Unit Economics

The model calculates:

**Cost per Call = Annual Fixed Rep Cost ÷ Annual Calls**

**Cost per Referral = Annual Fixed Rep Cost ÷ Annual Referrals**

**Fixed Cost per Admission = Annual Fixed Rep Cost ÷ Annual Admissions**

**Blended Cost per Admission = Total Annual Rep Cost ÷ Annual Admissions**

The blended measure includes commission. These figures help managers compare activity quality, conversion, compensation design, and productivity.

## 10. Break-Even and Profitability

The profitability model uses **net contribution per admission**: the contribution retained by the hospice after patient-care costs and before sales commission.

This distinction matters. Gross revenue does not show how much of an admission is available to cover the sales investment.

### 10.1 Monthly Break-Even Admissions

The model finds the smallest whole number of monthly admissions for which:

**Admissions × (Net Contribution per Admission − Applicable Commission per Admission) ≥ Monthly Fixed Rep Cost**

The solver evaluates whole admissions and applies the commission tier associated with each candidate volume. This avoids using the wrong commission rate when the break-even point crosses a tier boundary.

### 10.2 Annual Break-Even Admissions

The model finds the smallest whole number of annual admissions for which:

**Annual Admissions × (Net Contribution per Admission − Applicable Commission per Admission) ≥ Annual Fixed Rep Cost**

For annual evaluation, the applicable commission tier is determined from average monthly volume.

### 10.3 Contribution After Rep Cost

**Monthly Contribution After Rep Cost =**

**(Monthly Admissions × Net Contribution per Admission) − Monthly Commission − Monthly Fixed Rep Cost**

**Annual Contribution After Rep Cost = Monthly Contribution After Rep Cost × 12**

A positive result indicates that modeled contribution exceeds the modeled rep investment. A negative result indicates the rep has not reached cost coverage under the entered assumptions.

### 10.4 Important Interpretation

Break-even is a planning result, not an accounting opinion. Its quality depends on the accuracy of:

- Net contribution per admission.
- Salary and benefits assumptions.
- Mileage and fixed costs.
- Commission tiers.
- Activity and conversion assumptions.

Leaders should replace defaults with organization-specific figures and review the result with finance, operations, and clinical leadership.

## 11. Worked Example

The following example demonstrates the model mechanics. It is illustrative and should not be treated as a universal benchmark.

### Inputs

- Base salary: **$90,000**
- Benefits load: **42%**
- Annual business miles: **5,400**
- Other fixed costs: **$15,484**
- Calls per day: **12**
- Working days per month: **20**
- Calls per referral: **8**
- Referral-to-admission conversion: **70%**
- Net contribution per admission: **$5,000**
- Commission tiers: **$100 / $125 / $150**

### Activity Output

**Monthly calls:** 12 × 20 = **240**

**Annual calls:** 240 × 12 = **2,880**

**Monthly referrals:** 240 ÷ 8 = **30**

**Annual referrals:** 30 × 12 = **360**

**Monthly admissions:** 30 × 70% = **21**

**Annual admissions:** 21 × 12 = **252**

At 21 monthly admissions, the example reaches the third commission tier.

### Cost Output

**Benefits cost:** $90,000 × 42% = **$37,800**

Mileage is calculated using the configured rate in the active model.

**Annual fixed rep cost:** salary + benefits + mileage + other fixed costs

**Monthly commission:** 21 × $150 = **$3,150**

**Annual commission:** $3,150 × 12 = **$37,800**

**Total annual rep cost:** annual fixed rep cost + $37,800

### Profitability Output

**Monthly gross contribution before commission and rep cost:** 21 × $5,000 = **$105,000**

**Monthly contribution after rep cost:** $105,000 − $3,150 − monthly fixed rep cost

The calculator then identifies the first whole monthly and annual admission counts whose net contribution after applicable commission covers fixed rep cost.

### Management Interpretation

The example does not say that 21 admissions is automatically the correct target. It shows what the selected activity, conversion, contribution, and cost assumptions produce.

A manager should test:

- What happens if calls per referral improves?
- What happens if conversion rises from 70% to 75%?
- What happens if territory travel increases?
- What happens if actual net contribution differs by payer mix?
- What happens when monthly admissions cross a commission tier?

The model becomes valuable when leaders use it to compare scenarios and decide which operating lever to improve.

## 12. Manager Scorecard

The manager should review leading indicators, process quality, outcomes, and economics together.

### 12.1 Leading Indicators

- Priority accounts planned.
- Live referral-source conversations.
- Conversations per admission.
- Calls per referral.
- Weekly win condition progress.
- Pre-call plans completed.
- Objections practiced.
- Follow-ups completed on time.

### 12.2 Funnel Outcomes

- Referrals.
- Admissions.
- Referral-to-admission conversion.
- Lost admissions.
- Conversion loss signal.
- Referral-source mix.
- Movement from Discovery to Commitment.

### 12.3 Economic Outcomes

- Monthly and annual fixed rep cost.
- Commission cost.
- Total rep cost.
- Cost per call.
- Cost per referral.
- Fixed and blended cost per admission.
- Monthly and annual break-even admissions.
- Contribution after rep cost.

### 12.4 Quality and Access Outcomes

- Appropriateness of referrals.
- Speed to care.
- Clarity of referral pathways.
- Contact confidence.
- Reliability of follow-through.
- Patient-choice and privacy compliance.

No single metric should be managed in isolation. High activity with weak progression suggests a quality problem. Strong conversion with low activity may indicate an undersized pipeline. Admissions without ethical and clinical quality are not success.

## 13. Coaching Cadence

The model uses visible work and deliberate repetition.

### Daily

- Review the queue.
- Prepare the next account.
- Practice the hardest moment.
- Complete the visit.
- Debrief immediately.
- Schedule the next step.
- Record one tiny edge to improve.

### Weekly

- Segment and prioritize accounts.
- Set one weekly win condition.
- Build the route.
- Compare target conversations with actual conversations.
- Review movement through the four subjects.
- Rehearse the most important objection.
- Identify one performance lever for the next week.

### Monthly

- Review admissions goal and actual admissions.
- Recalculate personal conversations per admission.
- Review referral and conversion trends.
- Examine cost per call, referral, and admission.
- Compare modeled admissions with break-even admissions.
- Review contribution after rep cost.
- Reallocate territory effort where evidence supports a change.

### Quarterly

- Reassess account tiers and territory design.
- Validate contribution, cost, and compensation assumptions.
- Review payer, operational, and market changes.
- Identify skill themes across the team.
- Reset strategic account priorities.

Coaching should answer three questions:

1. What happened?
2. Why did it happen?
3. What will the representative do differently on the next repetition?

## 14. Diagnosing Performance

### High Activity, Low Referrals

Likely causes:

- Wrong accounts or contacts.
- Weak Discovery.
- Generic messaging.
- Insufficient relevance.
- Repeated visits without progression.

Coaching response:

- Revisit territory priorities.
- Audit contact profiles.
- Practice better questions.
- Define measurable outcomes for each visit.

### Adequate Referrals, Low Conversion

Likely causes:

- Inappropriate or poorly qualified referrals.
- Unclear eligibility understanding.
- Delayed response or handoff friction.
- Weak referral pathway.
- Operational barriers outside the representative’s control.

Coaching response:

- Review lost-referral reasons.
- Partner with clinical and operations leaders.
- Clarify triggers and the referral process.
- Improve speed and communication at handoff.

### Strong Relationships, Few Commitments

Likely causes:

- The representative remains in Connecting.
- Fear of asking for a defined next step.
- Education without operational application.
- No agreed referral trigger.

Coaching response:

- Practice the transition from Guiding to Commitment.
- Require an explicit next-step objective.
- Document the referral pathway.

### Admissions Below Break-Even

Likely causes:

- Insufficient activity.
- Low referral yield.
- Conversion leakage.
- Poor territory potential.
- High fixed-cost structure.
- Contribution assumption below plan.

Coaching response:

- Identify the largest controllable gap.
- Avoid demanding more activity until account quality and conversion are understood.
- Test scenarios in the rep-cost model.
- Align sales, finance, operations, and clinical leadership on the remedy.

## 15. Implementation Guide

### Phase 1: Establish Shared Definitions

Agree on:

- What counts as a live conversation.
- What counts as a referral.
- What counts as an admission.
- How conversion is calculated.
- Which costs belong in the rep model.
- How net contribution per admission is calculated.
- How commission tiers operate.

Shared formulas prevent numbers from being gamed.

### Phase 2: Baseline the Current State

Measure:

- Current admission goals.
- Historical conversations per admission.
- Calls per referral.
- Conversion rate.
- Territory composition.
- Rep cost.
- Contribution and break-even.

### Phase 3: Install the Four Subjects

Train one subject at a time. Require measurable outputs:

- Discovery profile.
- Connecting agreement.
- Guiding use case.
- Commitment pathway.

### Phase 4: Install the Daily Loop

Use the Command Center sequence:

**Queue → Pre-call → Practice → Visit → Debrief → Next Step**

### Phase 5: Coach the Evidence

Managers review actual account movement, actual conversations, actual conversion, and actual economics.

### Phase 6: Improve the System

Use results to:

- Reassign accounts.
- Improve routing.
- Change practice priorities.
- Remove referral friction.
- Update assumptions.
- Strengthen compensation alignment.

## 16. What the Spartan Sales Model Is Not

The model is not:

- A collection of generic scripts.
- A license to pressure clinicians or families.
- A leaderboard based only on visit counts.
- A replacement for clinical judgment.
- A guarantee of reimbursement or profitability.
- A substitute for finance, legal, compliance, or operational review.
- A reason to store protected patient information in sales tools.
- A one-time training event.

It is a repeatable management system for preparing, executing, measuring, coaching, and improving hospice sales work.

---

## 17. Final Standard

A Spartan sales organization can answer, with evidence:

- Which accounts matter and why.
- What each representative is trying to accomplish this week.
- Where each priority relationship sits in the four-subject model.
- How many live conversations are required to reach the admission goal.
- Where referrals are being gained or lost.
- What the representative costs.
- How many admissions are required to break even.
- Whether contribution exceeds the rep investment.
- What the manager is coaching next.
- How the work protects patient choice, clinical judgment, and privacy.

The standard is not motion. The standard is disciplined progress:

**Prepare with intent. Practice under pressure. Measure in the open. Correct fast. Finish strong.**
